# AI Without the Sales Pitch
## A six-class community education programme

### Series overview (for marketing and registration pages)

You have heard the word "AI" four thousand times. Nobody has sat you down and explained what it actually does on your laptop on a Tuesday afternoon. This series does that.

We teach the way the physicist Richard Feynman tested his own understanding: if you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it yet. So every idea arrives as an everyday picture before it gets a technical name. An AI model is your phone's autocomplete at enormous scale. Its "memory" is the size of the desk it works at. When it makes things up, it's a student bluffing an exam essay: it knows what a good answer sounds like, not what's true. By the end of each class, you'll be able to explain the idea to a colleague in three plain sentences, and that's how you'll know you've got it.

Over six classes, we take apart the hype without breathless enthusiasm. You'll learn what an AI model really is (a very large, very fast pattern-matcher made of numbers, not a thinking creature), how the big products differ (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot, and tools you can run on your own computer), and how to tell vendor marketing from a description of what the software does today, on your screen, with your data.

The classes are practical. Every session includes an exercise on your own device, and everyone leaves with ten ready-to-use documents: system prompt templates, an AI use policy to hand to your staff, a prompting cheat sheet, data security basics, a tool comparison table, an incident response card, and more.

We cover the questions that actually matter to a small business. What happens to the client data you paste into a chatbot? What does "training on your data" mean, and how do you opt out on each platform? What does the Australian Privacy Act expect of you? When does running AI on your own hardware make sense? And why is the collapsing cost of custom software a bigger deal for your business than any robot-takeover story?

No coding required. No jargon without an everyday picture and a plain translation. Bring a laptop or a phone, and bring your scepticism. We won't insult it.

Six classes. One evening each. Free to attend, registration required.
